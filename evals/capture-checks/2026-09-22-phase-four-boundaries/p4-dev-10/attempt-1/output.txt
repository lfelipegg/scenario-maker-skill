## Core distinction

**Local Krea 2 RAW/Turbo and hosted Krea 2 Medium/Large/Medium Turbo are distinct targets—not interchangeable names.** In particular, local Turbo should not be treated as hosted Medium Turbo.

The selected skill’s references document the local checkpoints and establish this boundary, but do not provide complete per-tier hosted specifications. The comparison below separates documented local behavior from hosted details that remain unverified.

| Area | Local Krea 2 RAW / Turbo | Hosted Krea 2 Medium / Large / Medium Turbo |
|---|---|---|
| **Prompt syntax** | Portable natural-language text is the recommended baseline. There is no official rigid clause order or required quality-token prefix. Quote exact visible lettering and clearly bind attributes and actions to their subjects. | The local profile does not establish separate syntax rules for these hosted tiers. Plain descriptive text is a conservative starting point, not proof that every hosted interface accepts the same special syntax. |
| **Length and structure** | The skill defaults to one cohesive paragraph of roughly 150 tokens. This is a writing preference—not a checkpoint requirement or token limit. Longer detailed prompts are recommended in the referenced guidance, but minimal prompts remain supported. | Do not transfer the local profile’s length default as a hosted requirement or assume different hosted sizes require different prompt lengths. No such per-tier rule is established here. |
| **Negative conditioning** | Support depends on the execution path. The official CLI exposes a positive prompt but no negative argument. The lower-level sampler accepts `negative_prompts` and uses that branch when guidance is greater than zero. Recommended Turbo guidance is `0.0`, which skips it. | Negative-prompt availability and actual consumption are not established by these references. Neither the hosted tier’s name nor local sampler support proves that a hosted UI or endpoint consumes negatives. |

Local sampler parameters such as CFG, steps, and `mu` are separate controls; no equivalence to hosted Creativity is established. | Creativity belongs to the hosted interface/control layer, not the text grammar. The supplied references do not establish its per-tier implementation or defaults. |

### Important local RAW–Turbo difference

RAW is the open **undistilled base checkpoint**; Turbo is the open **eight-step distilled checkpoint**. Their documented reference settings are:

- **RAW:** 52 steps, CFG 3.5.
- **Turbo:** 8 steps, CFG 0.0, `mu=1.15`.

These are **sampler settings, not words to insert into a prompt**. RAW’s positive guidance makes the lower-level negative-conditioning branch eligible, but it does not establish that a particular CLI, adapter, or graph supplies negative conditioning. A visible negative socket alone is insufficient evidence.

### Practical interpretation

The most portable component is the **scene description**: subjects, attribute bindings, relationships, composition, and exact quoted lettering. Special weighting syntax, negative channels, Creativity settings, and enhancement behavior should be assessed separately for the actual interface.

For enhancement, the optional local helper’s stated contract emphasizes faithful expansion, preserved medium and lettering, and no invented objects or unsupported specificity. That is an instruction to the helper—not a guarantee about an unknown downstream enhancer.

**Bottom line:** transfer clear descriptive content between local and hosted workflows, but do not transfer assumptions about controls or conditioning. The available documentation supports concrete RAW/Turbo distinctions; it does not support a claimed Medium-versus-Large-versus-Medium-Turbo feature matrix.
